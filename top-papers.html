2022 · Semantic Web · 317 citations

Neural Entity Linking: A Survey of Models Based on Deep Learning

Sevgili, O., Shelmanov, A., Arkhipov, M., Panchenko, A., and Biemann, C.

A sports report says that Wales beat San Marino. Which Wales should a search engine index: the country or the football team? This survey follows a mention from raw text to a knowledge-graph identifier. It compares how neural systems detect mentions, retrieve plausible entities and rank them using the surrounding text. The decomposition helps explain why a strong ranker can still return a wrong answer when retrieval omits the correct entity.

Research impact. The survey gives researchers a common vocabulary for comparing systems that make different assumptions about which parts of the problem are already solved.

Potential application. A news archive could link articles to teams and players. An editor testing it could first check whether the intended team entered the shortlist, then inspect which contextual clues caused it to win or lose.

2024 · Findings of ACL · 262 citations

Fact-Checking the Output of Large Language Models via Token-Level Uncertainty Quantification

Fadeeva, E., Rubashevskii, A., Shelmanov, A, Petrakov, S., Li, H., Mubarak, H., Tsymbalov, E., Kuzmin, G., Panchenko, A., Baldwin, T., Nakov, P., Panov, M.

One wrong name or date can spoil an otherwise accurate answer. This paper estimates uncertainty locally, using Claim-Conditioned Probability (CCP) to distinguish competing factual claims from alternative ways to express the same claim. The authors test whether the scores identify unsupported statements in generated biographies, comparing them with simpler probability-based measures.

Research impact. A reader can locate the specific claim that warrants a source check. Averaging confidence over a whole paragraph can conceal precisely that kind of error.

Potential application. An editor reviewing an automatically drafted biography could check the highlighted names and dates first, using external sources to decide whether they are correct.

2022 · ACL-22 · 220 citations

ParaDetox: Detoxification with Parallel Data

Logacheva, V., Dementieva, D., Ustyantsev, S., Moskovskiy, D., Dale, D, Krotova, I., Semenov, N., and Panchenko, A.

ParaDetox asks how to teach a model to remove an insult while preserving the point of a sentence. The researchers collected neutral rewrites for more than 10,000 English source sentences, separating the writing task from checks on meaning and toxicity. They also adapted their procedure to mine an existing paraphrase collection. Models trained on the resulting parallel data outperformed the unsupervised systems in the paper’s comparisons.

Research impact. The contribution is both a training resource and a repeatable collection method. Separate checks let researchers identify a rewrite that sounds polite but silently changes the author’s claim.

Potential application. A community platform developing a writing assistant could collect examples from its own domain, ask independent annotators to check suggested edits, and use the accepted pairs to train or evaluate its model.

2023 · EMNLP · 206 citations

LM-Polygraph: Uncertainty Estimation for Language Models

Fadeeva, E., Vashurin, R., Tsvigun, R., Vazhentsev, A., Petrakov, S., Fedyanin, K., Vasilev, D., Goncharova, E., Panchenko, A., Panov, M., Baldwin, T., Shelmanov, A.

LM-Polygraph puts uncertainty estimators behind a shared interface. Some estimators read token probabilities or internal model signals. Others compare several sampled answers and can work when only generated text is available. The library and interactive demo make it possible to run an estimator on a generation and inspect how its score relates to output quality.

Research impact. A common implementation makes comparisons reproducible and exposes practical differences such as whether an estimator needs extra generations or access to model internals.

Potential application. A support team could test several estimators on previously resolved questions and measure how many incorrect answers each would route to a human reviewer at a fixed review budget.

2025 · Transactions of the ACL · 204 citations

Benchmarking Uncertainty Quantification Methods for Large Language Models with LM-Polygraph

Roman Vashurin, Ekaterina Fadeeva, Artem Vazhentsev, Akim Tsvigun, Daniil Vasilev, Rui Xing, Abdelrahman “Boda” Sadallah, Lyudmila Rvanova, Sergey Petrakov, Alexander Panchenko, Timothy Baldwin, Preslav Nakov, Maxim Panov, Artem Shelmanov

This follow-up asks how well uncertainty scores transfer across models and tasks. The authors evaluate competing estimators under a common protocol, separating question answering from other generation tasks and black-box access from settings with internal probabilities. Selective prediction tests whether quality improves when the system withholds the outputs it considers most uncertain.

Research impact. The benchmark provides a way to choose an estimator for a particular task and access level. A result obtained with white-box probabilities may have little bearing on a service that exposes text alone.

Potential application. An application developer could compare candidate scores under the same API constraints as a production assistant, then validate a threshold on examples drawn from its intended audience.

2023 · EMNLP · 200 citations

Kandinsky: an Improved Text-to-Image Synthesis with Image Prior and Latent Diffusion

Razzhigaev, A., Shakhmatov, A., Maltseva, A., Arkhipkin, V., Pavlov, I., Ryabov, I. Kuts, A., Panchenko, A., Kuznetsov, A., Dimitrov, D.

Kandinsky separates the meaning of a prompt from the process that renders an image. A prior maps the conditioning input into an image-embedding space. A diffusion decoder then synthesizes an image in a compressed latent space. The paper illustrates how this split supports both text-driven generation and operations that use existing images as conditioning inputs.

Research impact. The same decoder can be used to explore several kinds of visual control. The examples make it possible to see what changes when the input representation is altered.

Potential application. A designer could generate candidate illustrations from a brief, then use a reference image or an interpolation to explore variations before selecting a composition for manual refinement.

2016 · RepL4NLP · Before S-NLP · 200 citations

Making Sense of Word Embeddings

Maria Pelevina, Nikolay Arefiev, Chris Biemann, Alexander Panchenko

A single word vector puts multiple senses at one location. The authors build a local graph around a target word, connect related neighbors, and cluster that graph to induce senses. Each cluster yields its own vector. The surrounding words in a sentence can then select a sense, and the authors evaluate this procedure against unsupervised word-sense disambiguation systems.

Research impact. The clusters expose which neighboring words distinguish one reading from another. They also let researchers reuse a pretrained embedding space without first constructing a hand-labeled sense inventory.

Potential application. A search tool could suggest furniture-related expansions for “table” in a furnishing catalogue and data-related expansions in a database manual.

2020 · CLEF · 190 citations

Overview of Touché 2020: Argument Retrieval

Bondarenko, A., Fröbe, M., Beloucif, M., Gienapp, L., Ajjour, Y., Panchenko, A., Biemann, C., Stein, B., Wachsmuth, H., Potthast, M. Hagen, M.

The first Touché lab turned two search needs into separate retrieval tasks. One asks for arguments on a public controversy. The other asks for evidence useful to someone comparing alternatives. The organizers provided topic descriptions and relevance criteria so systems could be evaluated on whether a passage actually addressed the user’s decision.

Research impact. A shared benchmark lets researchers compare argument-aware retrieval with ordinary lexical search while keeping the queries and evaluation criteria fixed.

Potential application. A comparison service could use the rent-or-buy topic to test whether its results explain conditions favoring each option, rather than merely containing both words.

2019 · ACL-19 Demo · 135 citations

TARGER: Neural Argument Mining at Your Fingertips

Chernodub, A., Oliynyk, O., Heidenreich, P., Bondarenko, A., Hagen, M., Biemann, C., Panchenko, A.

TARGER connects two ways of reading arguments. A reader can submit a passage to a pretrained sequence tagger and inspect its predicted argument spans. Alternatively, a reader can search a web corpus whose passages have already been tagged. The paper describes models trained on three argument-mining datasets, provides a web interface and API, and explores using argumentative content as a search-ranking signal.

Research impact. Sharing both the models and a searchable collection made it easier to test a retrieval idea without first training a tagger or annotating a web corpus.

Potential application. A policy analyst could search for passages about a proposed measure, inspect the claims and premises in context, and follow the original documents to assess the evidence.

2021 · EMNLP · 127 citations

Text Detoxification using Large Pre-trained Neural Models

Dale, D., Voronov, A., Dementieva, D., Logacheva, V., Kozlova, O., Semenov, N. and Panchenko, A.

The authors compare two ways to revise offensive phrasing while keeping the underlying message. ParaGeDi guides a paraphraser as it generates a new sentence. CondBERT identifies potentially toxic words, proposes replacements of variable length and ranks them for compatibility with the original. The evaluation considers toxicity reduction, meaning preservation and fluency together, since a bland or unrelated sentence can easily receive a low toxicity score.

Research impact. The comparison makes a practical design choice visible: regenerating an entire sentence gives the model freedom to rephrase, while editing a short span leaves more of the author’s wording intact.

Potential application. A discussion forum could offer an optional rewrite before a comment is posted. Showing the draft alongside the suggestion lets its author check whether the system preserved the criticism they meant to express.

2021 · CLEF · 126 citations

Overview of Touché 2021: Argument Retrieval

Bondarenko, A., Gienapp, L., Fröbe, M., Beloucif, M., Ajjour,Y., Panchenko, A., Biemann,C., Stein, B., Wachsmuth, H., Potthast, M., Hagen, M.

The second Touché lab returned to controversial questions and everyday comparisons. It evaluated the relevance of retrieved material and the quality of the argument it contained. The organizers report that several participating systems improved on argument-agnostic baselines, while the detailed rankings reveal that success on one criterion does not guarantee success on the other.

Research impact. Separating relevance from argument quality makes an important failure measurable: a passage can be on topic while offering a weak reason for its conclusion.

Potential application. A public consultation portal could evaluate a search update twice, first for retrieving responses on the issue and then for surfacing responses that clearly explain their reasoning.

2016 · SemEval · Before S-NLP · 116 citations

TAXI at SemEval-2016 Task 13: A Taxonomy Induction Method Based on Lexico-Syntactic Patterns, Substrings and Focused Crawling

Alexander Panchenko, Stefano Faralli, Eugen Ruppert, Steffen Remus, Hubert Naets, Cédrick Fairon, Simone Paolo Ponzetto, Chris Biemann

Given a list of domain terms, TAXI seeks broader terms that can organize them into a hierarchy. It extracts evidence from linguistic patterns and from words embedded in longer expressions, supplementing general corpora with focused web crawling. The SemEval evaluation tests whether the links produced by these signals reconstruct useful taxonomies across several domains.

Research impact. The paper connects individual extraction decisions to the structure of the resulting hierarchy. Its precision–recall analysis shows why gathering more candidate links can also introduce poor parent assignments.

Potential application. A specialist glossary editor could use the proposed broader terms as candidates when adding a new entry, reviewing the supporting text before accepting a link.

2024 · CLEF · 103 citations

Overview of PAN 2024: Multi-Author Writing Style Analysis, Multilingual Text Detoxification, Oppositional Thinking Analysis, and Generative AI Authorship Verification (Condensed Lab Overview)

Abinew Ali Ayele, Nikolay Babakov, Janek Bevendorff, Xavier Bonet Casals, Berta Chulvi, Daryna Dementieva, Ashaf Elnagar, Dayne Freitag, Maik Fröbe, Damir Korenčić, Maximilian Mayerl, Daniil Moskovskiy, Animesh Mukherjee, Alexander Panchenko, Martin Potthast, Francisco Rangel, Naquee Rizwan, Paolo Rosso, Florian Schneider, Alisa Smirnova, Efstathios Stamatatos, Elisei Stakovskii, Benno Stein, Mariona Taulé, Dmitry Ustalov, Xintong Wang, Matti Wiegmann, Seid Muhie Yimam & Eva Zangerle

PAN 2024 brought four evaluation problems together: changes of writing style within multi-author documents, rewriting toxic text in multiple languages, identifying elements of oppositional narratives, and verifying generative-AI authorship. The condensed overview explains what participants had to predict or generate, how data were prepared, and how systems were assessed for each task.

Research impact. The task definitions make the unit of evaluation explicit. Locating a style boundary, labeling a narrative span and preserving the meaning of a rewrite call for different evidence and evaluation criteria.

Potential application. A multilingual writing-tool team could adopt the detoxification track’s evaluation setup to compare rewrites language by language. A media researcher could separately reuse the narrative annotation scheme to study how actors are portrayed.

2018 · Dialogue 2015 · 2018 preprint · Before S-NLP · 90 citations

RUSSE: The First Workshop on Russian Semantic Similarity

Alexander Panchenko, Natalia Loukachevitch, Dmitry Ustalov, Denis Paperno, Christian Meyer, Natalia Konstantinova

RUSSE evaluated semantic similarity for Russian nouns at Dialogue 2015. The report, posted as a preprint in 2018, compares 105 submissions from 19 teams across four benchmarks. It contrasts human similarity judgments with tests derived from lexical relations and associations. Supervised combinations performed strongly, but a skip-gram model trained on a large corpus was also competitive.

Research impact. The benchmarks reveal how differently a system can behave when asked for a similar word versus an associated one. They gave Russian-language researchers a common basis for comparing models.

Potential application. A Russian search engine could test candidate query expansions on the benchmark that best matches its purpose, then inspect pairs that its model ranks highly despite weak human similarity ratings.

2025 · SemEval · 89 citations

SemEval-2025 Task 11: Bridging the Gap in Text-Based Emotion Detection

Muhammad, S. H., Ousidhoum, N., Abdulmumin, I., Yimam, S. M., Wahle, J. P., Ruas, T., Beloucif, M., De Kock, C., Belay, T. D., Ahmad, I. S., Surange, N., Teodorescu, D., Adelani, D. I., Aji, A. F., Ali, F., Araujo, V., Ayele, A. A., Ignat, O., Panchenko, A., Zhou, Y., Mohammad, S. M.

SemEval-2025 Task 11 asked systems to infer perceived emotions from text in more than 30 languages. The three tracks examine multi-label emotion detection, intensity prediction and cross-lingual transfer. A passage can express several emotions, and a model trained on one set of languages may need to predict labels in another. The report compares participating systems under these different conditions.

Research impact. Multilingual evaluation reveals gaps that an English-only score would hide. The distinction between detecting a category and estimating its intensity also changes how errors should be interpreted.

Potential application. A multilingual feedback-analysis team could test whether its model recognizes frustration or sadness in written comments across languages, then check its interpretations against local human judgments.

2021 · EACL · 87 citations

How Certain is Your Transformer?

Shelmanov, A., Tsymbalov, E., Puzyrev, D., Fedyanin, K., Panchenko, A., Panov, M.

The paper tests whether varying dropout masks at inference can reveal when a Transformer classifier is likely to make a mistake. Multiple stochastic predictions provide a measure of disagreement. The authors compare ways to sample the masks and investigate how restricting dropout to later layers affects error detection and runtime.

Research impact. The study treats inference cost as part of the evaluation. This matters when a small improvement in error detection requires several extra predictions per input.

Potential application. A text-routing service could run a cheaper uncertainty estimate first and send ambiguous cases to a human queue, measuring both missed errors and added latency before adoption.

2021 · EACL · 83 citations

Active Learning for Sequence Tagging with Deep Pre-trained Models and Bayesian Uncertainty Estimates

Shelmanov, A., Puzyrev, D., Kupriyanova, L., Belyakov, D., Larionov, D., Khromov, N., Kozlova, O., Artemova, E. Dylov, D. V., and Panchenko A.

Sequence tagging requires annotators to label spans throughout a sentence. This paper studies an active-learning loop that chooses which unlabeled sentences to annotate next using a pretrained model and uncertainty estimates. It compares acquisition strategies, dropout configurations and random sampling, tracking tagging quality as the annotation budget grows.

Research impact. Learning curves make it possible to compare annotation strategies at the same labeling budget. The study also examines whether examples selected with one model help train a successor model.

Potential application. A team creating a named-entity corpus could label an initial batch, use uncertainty to choose the next sentences, and periodically compare its tagging performance against a randomly sampled baseline.

2016 · AIST · Before S-NLP · 83 citations

Human and Machine Judgements for Russian Semantic Relatedness

Alexander Panchenko, Dmitry Ustalov, Nikolay Arefyev, Denis Paperno, Natalia Konstantinova, Natalia Loukachevitch, Chris Biemann

The authors assemble five Russian lexical resources. Four evaluate semantic relatedness from different perspectives. The fifth is a high-coverage distributional thesaurus built using a strong method identified through the shared evaluation. Native-speaker judgments are used to assess the quality of its neighbors. The example for “physics” shows what a user actually finds in the resulting resource.

Research impact. The paper connects a benchmark result to a usable lexical resource and checks the generated neighbors directly. That second step matters because good aggregate scores need not imply equally useful entries for every word.

Potential application. A lexicographer could inspect ranked neighbors when drafting a domain vocabulary, using the human judgments and frequency breakdown to decide which suggestions need closer review.

2022 · ACL-22 · 82 citations

Uncertainty Estimation of Transformer Predictions for Misclassification Detection

Vazhentsev, A., Kuzmin, G., Shelmanov, A., Tsvigun, A., Tsymbalov, E., Fedyanin, K., Panov, M., Panchenko, A., Gusev, G., Burtsev, M., Avetisian, M., and Zhukov, L.

This study compares ways to predict when a Transformer classifier or token tagger will make an error. It evaluates stochastic methods that use multiple predictions alongside deterministic methods that can score examples in one pass. The authors compare performance across datasets and examine what happens when a system rejects its least confident outputs.

Research impact. The comparison separates the quality of error ranking from the computational expense of obtaining it. A method that works well for one dataset or one model may rank differently on another.

Potential application. A document-processing team could use rejection curves to estimate how much accuracy improves when a fixed fraction of predictions is sent for review, then check the same trade-off on its own data.

2013 · TextGraphs · Before S-NLP · 75 citations

A Graph-Based Approach to Skill Extraction from Text

Ilkka Kivimäki, Alexander Panchenko, Adrien Dessy, Dries Verdegem, Pascal Francq, Hugues Bersini, Marco Saerens

This system infers professional skills associated with a document. It first identifies Wikipedia articles related to the text, then spreads activation through the hyperlink graph toward candidate skill concepts. A bias against popular hubs helps keep broadly connected pages from dominating. The evaluation examines choices in both text matching and graph traversal.

Research impact. The graph can connect a document to a skill even when its preferred label does not appear verbatim. Inspecting the intermediate Wikipedia concepts gives the reader a way to understand the association.

Potential application. An internal knowledge base could suggest skill tags for technical reports and ask their authors to confirm them, making it easier for colleagues to find relevant project experience.

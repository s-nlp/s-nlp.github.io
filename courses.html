Courses

Our group designed and teaches two courses for master’s and PhD students.

Introduction to Natural Language Processing

3 ECTS

This course gives a broad introduction to NLP through the linguistic concepts behind its applications. Students explore how language-processing systems represent words, analyze sentence structure, and interpret meaning in context. The course follows Dan Jurafsky and James H. Martin’s textbook Speech and Language Processing.

Transformers and Large Language Models

3 ECTS

This course explores the Transformer architecture, its variants, and their use in language and multimodal models. Lectures and practical sessions connect architectural choices to applications including dialogue, retrieval-augmented generation, and tool-using agents. Students also examine long-context inference and the trade-offs involved in running models locally.

Papers about the course

  • Industry vs Academia: Running a Course on Transformers in Two Setups. Nikishina et al., Sixth Workshop on Teaching NLP (2024). The instructors describe how they adapted lectures, practical sessions, and competition-based assignments for university and industry audiences.
  • From Standard Transformers to Modern LLMs: Bringing Dialogue Models, RAG, and Agents to the Classroom. Tikhonova et al., Seventh Workshop on Teaching Natural Language Processing (2026). The updated course introduces retrieval, tool use, multimodal dialogue, and inference under latency and cost constraints.
